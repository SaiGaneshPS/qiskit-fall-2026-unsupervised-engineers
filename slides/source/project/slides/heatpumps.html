Heat pumps: which homes switch first matters

40k oil homes to heat pumps

-260 kt CO2 a year, but blackout hours 1.6 to 2.7 (limit 2.8). Keep the furnace for below -10 C: 2.1.

50k baseboard homes to heat pumps

Blackout hours halved, 1.6 to 0.8: a zero-emission power plant made of living rooms.

Here is the “ahh yes” moment. Switching oil furnaces to heat pumps is great for the climate, about 260,000 tonnes of CO2 a year for 40,000 homes, but it adds electric load exactly on the coldest nights, when heat pumps are least efficient. Expected blackout hours jump from 1.6 to 2.7, right up against the limit. Keeping the old furnace for the coldest hours fixes most of it. And the wow: switching baseboard homes to heat pumps does the opposite. Same heat, less electricity, so blackout risk is cut in half. Program order matters.
