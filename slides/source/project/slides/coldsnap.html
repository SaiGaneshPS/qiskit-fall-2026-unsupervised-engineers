A judge might say: a classical convolution solves your single-hour problem instantly, so why quantum? Because real cold snaps last days and outages persist; Bay d’Espoir was down four days in January 2026. We extended the circuit in time: each plant gets a qubit per day whose rotation depends on its own state the day before, plus a severity register learned from the worst cold snap of each of the 46 winters. Two things come out. Treating days as independent, which is what makes the fast classical method fast, overstates the risk by half. And the circuit grows linearly with days and plants, while exact enumeration explodes, which is why planners sample, and sampling is what amplitude estimation speeds up. The persistence parameters are illustrative.
