2030: Holyrood’s oil can retire without new diesel, if…

…the Labrador-Island Link keeps its measured reliability. The whole decision hinges on one rare event.

Link at 1.1% (measured): Bay d’Espoir U8 + 300 MW wind + 50k heat pumps gives 1.6 h, better than the diesel plan’s 1.9 h. Add 40k oil homes: 2.0 h, 883 kt CO2 a year avoided.

Link at 5% (Hydro’s planning value): nothing tested meets 2.8 h, the diesel plan included (8.7 h).

First winter without Holyrood’s steam units; peak forecast 1,860 MW (NL Hydro). Screening model: no transmission limits, hydro energy limits or imports.

The decision. Retiring Holyrood with nothing added is far outside the criterion. Hydro’s plan adds a new diesel turbine and a hydro unit. Our clean alternative uses no new diesel: Bay d’Espoir Unit 8, 300 megawatts of wind and 50,000 baseboard-to-heat-pump conversions. If the Labrador-Island Link keeps the 1.1 percent outage rate it has actually achieved, the clean portfolio is more reliable than the diesel plan, and adding oil-home conversions avoids almost 900,000 tonnes of CO2 a year. If the link fails as often as Hydro’s planning assumes, nothing we tested passes. So everything hinges on a rare-event probability, and those are exactly what the quantum component estimates.
