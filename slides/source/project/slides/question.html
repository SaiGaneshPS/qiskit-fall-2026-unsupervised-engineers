If we turn the oil off and switch homes to heat pumps, will the lights stay on in the next cold snap?

The standard

At most 2.8 expected blackout hours a year (NL Hydro’s criterion).

A rare event

That is about one winter hour in 1,700 short of power, too rare to see often in a simulation.

The catch

The safer the grid, the more simulated hours it takes to prove it: about 170,000 per option for a 10% answer.

Here is the question everyone understands. Utilities turn it into a number: expected blackout hours per year, which must stay under 2.8. For today’s grid that means a shortfall in roughly one winter hour in 1,700. Planners estimate it by simulating random hours, Monte Carlo. Rare events are what Monte Carlo is worst at: to get one option to 10 percent accuracy you need about 170,000 simulated hours, and the safer the grid, the more you need. That is where a quantum algorithm with a provable speedup for estimating probabilities fits.
